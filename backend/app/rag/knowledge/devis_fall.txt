LOCATION: Devi's Fall Overlook (devis_fall)

## Overview
Devi's Fall — locally known as Patale Chhango, meaning "Underground Waterfall" or
"Netherworld Falls" in Nepali — is one of Pokhara's most famous natural landmarks,
located in Ward 17, Birauta / Chhorepatan area, about 2 km southwest of Pokhara
Airport. It is unusual among waterfalls because the water does not continue as a
surface river afterward — it vanishes completely underground.

## Source of the water
The waterfall is fed by the Pardi Khola stream, which itself is fed by an overflow
outlet of Phewa Lake (Fewa Tal), Pokhara's largest lake. During and after the
monsoon season (June–August), the volume of water increases dramatically, and the
sound of water crashing into the sinkhole becomes deafening. During the dry season
(roughly October–May), the flow is much gentler and quieter.

## The sinkhole and geological history
At the site, the Pardi Khola plunges into a natural sinkhole and disappears into an
underground tunnel system. Estimates of the tunnel's depth vary by source, ranging
from roughly 100 feet (about 30 m) below the surface to a total underground channel
length of around 500 feet (about 150 m) before the water re-emerges. The disappearing
water re-enters daylight far downstream, having traveled beneath the Gupteshwor
Mahadev Cave complex on the opposite side of the road. The sinkhole itself is a karst
feature — Pokhara Valley sits on limestone bedrock that is highly prone to dissolution,
producing sinkholes, underground rivers, and cave systems throughout the region. In
fact, over 200 sinkholes were documented forming in the nearby Armala area between
2013 and 2017, some over 7 meters deep, illustrating how geologically active this karst
landscape still is.

## The name and its history
The name "Devi's Fall" (also spelled "Davis Fall" or "David's Fall") comes from a
tragic incident on July 31, 1961. A Swiss couple were near the site — accounts vary
between them bathing in Phewa Lake or swimming near the stream — when a sudden
overflow (some accounts attribute this to the Phewa Dam gates opening unexpectedly)
swept a woman into the sinkhole. She drowned, and her body was recovered three days
later downstream in the Phusre Khola river. Local people began calling the site
"Davis Falls" (or "Devi's Falls," which also resonates with "Devi," a Nepali/Sanskrit
word for goddess) in her memory. This is the most widely repeated account, though
exact spellings of her name and precise details differ between sources — it is treated
locally as legend as much as documented history.

## Safety measures
Since the tragedy, the site has been developed with safety barriers, viewing platforms,
and railings along the edges of the sinkhole to keep visitors a safe distance from the
water and the drop. Entry to Devi's Fall itself is free of charge (unlike the cave
across the road, which charges an entrance fee). The site is open daily, roughly from
morning to evening.

## Visiting tips
- Best visual drama is during monsoon season (June–August) when flow is highest.
- Quieter, less crowded visits are best in the off-season (September–May).
- Morning or late afternoon light is best for photography.
- The site connects directly across the road to Gupteshwor Mahadev Cave — visitors
  typically see both in the same trip, since the underground channel physically links
  the two.
