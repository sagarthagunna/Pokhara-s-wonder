LOCATION: Cave Entrance Plaza (entrance_plaza)

## Overview
The Cave Entrance Plaza is the surface-level gateway to Gupteshwor Mahadev Cave,
sitting directly across the road from Devi's Fall in Pokhara-17, Chhorepatan, Kaski
District. This is where visitors buy tickets, browse souvenir stalls, and — for this
tour — rent the gear needed to safely continue into the Deep Cave Shivalaya.

## Ticket pricing
Entry to Gupteshwor Mahadev Cave requires a ticket, purchased at a counter near the
plaza entrance. As of recent years, the standard price is:
- NPR 100 for non-Nepali (foreign) visitors
- Roughly half that (around NPR 50) for Nepali citizens
Tickets are checked again at the bottom of the entrance staircase, so visitors are
advised to purchase before descending, not after.

## Cave length statistics
Gupteshwor Mahadev Cave is regarded as one of the longest caves in Nepal. Different
surveys report different figures — most commonly cited lengths are approximately
2,057 meters (per some cave databases) up to around 2,950 meters (per popular local
and tourism sources). This variation reflects the difficulty of fully surveying a wet,
active limestone cave system with flooded and collapsed sections. It was first formally
surveyed by British and German speleological expeditions: Tony Waltham and colleagues
surveyed it as part of the British Karst Research Expedition to the Himalaya, and
German speleologist Herbert Daniel Gebauer documented sections during the first German
speleological expedition to the Himalaya in 1986, noting the cave's karst features in
Pokhara Valley's limestone formations.

## Historical discovery stories
Local legend places the cave's discovery in the 16th century, when young local
villagers clearing grass in the area reportedly stumbled into an opening in the ground.
As they explored deeper, they said they found a naturally-formed rock structure
resembling a Shivalinga (a symbol of the Hindu god Shiva). This was later formally
consecrated and installed near the cave's mouth — some sources date this installation
to 1992. Other, more modern sources date the cave's "discovery" (in the sense of local
rediscovery/popularization) to 1950. What is well documented is that the main
constructed entrance — the staircase and archway visitors use today — was not built
until 1991, meaning the cave existed as a wild, informally-accessed site for centuries
before infrastructure was added.

## What's at the plaza
- A large decorative stone archway marks the entrance.
- Numerous souvenir stalls line the approach (bargaining is customary).
- A statue of Lord Vishnu in a reclining position greets visitors near the ticket area.
- A concrete balcony and spiral staircase lead down toward the cave mouth.
- This is also the designated point for renting a flashlight and safety helmet before
  continuing into the Deep Cave Shivalaya — the deeper, unlit, and physically
  demanding second section of the cave is considered unsafe without this gear.

## Practical notes
- The cave's deeper second section is sometimes closed entirely during monsoon season,
  when water from the waterfall floods parts of the passage.
- Photography is prohibited in some of the deeper shrine areas, monitored by security
  guards.
- Paths beyond the plaza are consistently damp and slippery.
